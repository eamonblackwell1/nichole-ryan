Just you and Ryan, no schedule, no group to manage. We plan the first week down to the minute. The second week, we leave open by design.

Wedding Week Itinerary

The family week.

The first week is structured enough that everyone knows where to be, but not so packed that the trip feels like a tour bus schedule. Tokyo gives everyone a soft landing, Kyoto brings the cultural center of the trip, Kobe anchors the wedding, and Osaka gives the family one final night together before departures.

Days 1–3

Tokyo arrival and culture

  • Family arrives, transfers are handled, hotel check-in is simple, and the first night stays intentionally light.
  • One Tokyo day is built around Ryan: Akihabara, Nintendo Tokyo, gaming/anime stops, Mario Kart, and ramen.
  • One Tokyo day is built around Nichole: Senso-ji, Meiji Shrine, architecture, gardens, and a quieter cultural pace.
  • The families come together over a private dinner before the trip moves west.
Days 4–5

Kyoto and the welcome dinner

  • Group Shinkansen from Tokyo to Kyoto, with luggage and seating handled in advance.
  • Kyoto gives the family the cultural center of the trip: temples, Gion, tea, architecture, and traditional food.
  • The welcome dinner becomes the first real wedding-week moment where everyone settles in together.
Days 6–7

Kobe and the wedding

  • Short transfer from Kyoto to Kobe, then final prep, venue walkthrough, and rehearsal dinner.
  • The wedding day is fully managed: hair, makeup, vendor coordination, timing, guest support, and Japanese-language logistics.
  • The ceremony blends Western structure with Japanese meaning: processional, vows, light Christian phrasing, san-san-kudo, rings, and reception.
  • Dinner, speeches, first dance, dancing, and optional kimono reception entrance all sit inside the same clear flow.
Days 8–9

Osaka and departures

  • The morning after the wedding stays slow, then the group transfers to Osaka for food, neon, and a softer final day.
  • A farewell dinner gives the families one last shared moment before everyone heads home.
  • Departures from Kansai International are coordinated so transfers, bags, and timing are handled.
  • Nichole and Ryan continue into the open-ended honeymoon week.

Sorakuen · Kobe

~$25,700 USD wedding day

Sorakuen is a heritage Japanese garden in central Kobe with a restored Western-style mansion at its heart. It is exactly the fusion you described. The architecture is unmistakably Japanese, the gardens give you the nature backdrop you want as the focal point, and the indoor reception space inside the heritage mansion is formal enough for tuxedos and floor-length gowns without feeling stiff.

Cherry blossoms in Kobe peak in early April, in the same window as Kyoto. The Sorakuen garden itself has cherry trees throughout. Catering is French cuisine with Japanese technique, prepared by an in-house team that has been doing this for decades. Kobe sits under an hour from Kyoto by Shinkansen, which is what makes the multi-city flow of the family week work as well as it does.

Sentir La Saison Kobayashiro · Niigata

~$24,000–27,000 USD wedding day

Kobayashiro is a hundred-year-old Japanese heritage estate in Mitsuke City, Niigata. It was founded in the Taisho era as a ryotei, the kind of high-end traditional restaurant where statesmen and merchants were entertained, and the estate has been preserved in classical Japanese style ever since. The chapel inside the estate is a two-storey atrium with windows that open onto the garden, so the ceremony happens with the garden as the literal backdrop.

What makes Kobayashiro different from the other venues on this list is exclusivity. They do one wedding per day. The entire estate, all 1000 tsubo of garden, the heritage buildings, the chapel, the reception hall, all of it, is yours alone for the day. Most Japanese wedding venues run multiple weddings concurrently. This one does not. For a 25-person wedding party, it means the place feels like your private estate for the day.

The cuisine is kaiseki with French technique, all served with chopsticks, using local Niigata produce and Hokkaido seafood. Niigata is on the Japan Sea side, two hours by Shinkansen from Tokyo. Cherry blossoms peak early-to-mid April. Almost no foreign visitor would end up here without a reason, which is exactly the "real Japan" you described.

One note on geography: Niigata is north of Tokyo, not on the Kansai route. If you choose Kobayashiro, the family week itinerary restructures to Tokyo first, then Niigata for the wedding, then back through Tokyo before family departures. We would adjust the trip flow to match.

Hotel Epinard Nasu · Tochigi

~$20,000–22,000 USD wedding day

Hotel Epinard Nasu sits in a 40-acre forest in the Nasu highlands of Tochigi prefecture, two and a half hours from Tokyo by Shinkansen plus shuttle. The chapel is a small wooden structure with a triangular roof and a cross, surrounded entirely by trees. It is the closest thing to the "magical backdrop rooted in nature" that you described, and we have not seen anything else in Japan that captures it the same way.

Two specific moments make this venue different from anything else on this list. First, an alpaca from the neighboring animal park can deliver your wedding rings during the ceremony. We mention it because it is unusual and memorable and the photos are absurdly good. Skip it if it is not your style. Second, after the ceremony there is a balloon release into the forest, and at nightfall the chapel can be lit entirely by candles. Your guests stay onsite at the resort, which solves the entire transport question for a 25-person wedding party.

Cherry blossoms in Nasu peak mid-to-late April. The Imperial family uses Nasu as a highland retreat. This is real Japan, not tourist Japan, and almost no foreign visitor would end up here without a reason.

The Tokyo Option

Togo Kinenkan · Tokyo

~$20,000–24,000 USD wedding day

Togo Kinenkan is a heritage Japanese garden venue in the heart of Harajuku, Tokyo. It belongs to the Togo Shrine, which honors a turn-of-the-century admiral, and the gardens have been preserved in classical Japanese style for over a hundred years. The reception hall, Tensho, looks out over the garden through floor-to-ceiling glass, which means you get a Japanese garden view from your dinner table.

The advantage of Togo Kinenkan is that nobody has to travel for the wedding. Your family stays in their Tokyo hotel. The wedding happens in Tokyo. The cultural day before or after is a short walk through Harajuku and Omotesando. For a 25-person wedding party where the elders and grandmother are coming, this is the simplest logistics by a wide margin.

One caveat: the Tensho hall typically operates with a 60-person minimum on food and drink. We have reached out to the venue to confirm whether a smaller hall option is available for your group size, or whether the minimum can be flexed for a destination wedding. We will know before the meeting.

For Your Honeymoon Week

Some directions, no plan.

Ryan wants to wing it, which we respect. But "real Japan" is a big country and a few of these regions are worth knowing exist before you start improvising. No bookings, no schedule. Just options.

Hakone or Kawaguchiko

Hot springs in sight of Mount Fuji. A few days of slow-paced ryokan stays, mountain hikes, and traditional Japanese inn dining. Easy from Tokyo.

Naoshima & the Art Islands

Tiny islands in the Seto Inland Sea covered in modern art installations. Photogenic, quiet, and unlike anywhere else in Japan. A two or three day trip.

Tohoku Region

Northern Honshu. Snow country, samurai towns, hot springs. Almost no foreign tourists. The "real Japan" you described, in its purest form.

Okinawa

If you want a beach pivot at the end. Tropical, distinct from mainland Japan, two-and-a-half hours from Tokyo by flight. Different language and culture.
